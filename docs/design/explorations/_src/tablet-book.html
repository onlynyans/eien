3 · An open book

The room goes to pages, not panes. The note you came from, the phone’s ⇄, gets a page of its own and faces the one you’re in, as Facing pages does on the desktop. On the fold the crease is the gutter. The contents come to you: the bar’s back word slides them over the page.

Intent
The writer drafting a chapter beside its notes (chapter iv beside Erwin’s sheet), on a foldable held open like a paperback or a tablet on the knee. They write in one and glance at the other. It should feel like an open book on the desk at night, with the fold as its gutter.
Palette
Washi’s tokens. One sheet of paper on the blotter, and the ribbon in ink-soft, which is Facing pages’ decided mark. Vermilion only in the seal’s places: the caret, ▪ sealed, the chosen row in the contents.
Depth
One sheet with the sheet’s shadow, opened at a soft-ruled gutter. On the fold the sheet is the screen and the crease is its gutter. The contents slide over as raised paper with the float shadow, because they float above the page.
Surfaces
The blotter round the sheet on a tablet, the bar on the desk, and the drawer raised over the scrim. Darker is further back, by Day and by Night.
Type
Each page in the text face. A leaf a phone’s width or narrower (the fold’s halves, an upright tablet) sets its words a size down, as the phone does. The folio, the crumbs and the back word are in the machine voice.
Spacing
The gutter is --eien-space-6, as decided for Facing pages, or the crease itself on the fold. The folio is a finger high (44px), so its × is a real target.

Try

  • Screen: Folded shows the phone: What the Recruits Say, with ⇄ Erwin in the bar. Open it, and Erwin faces the chapter across the fold.
  • Tap the facing page to write there: the ribbon moves. ⇄ in the bar swaps the pages.
  • ‹ Beneath the Walls slides the list over the page. A tap opens the note on the page you’re in, and long-press gives “Open on the facing page”.
  • A page’s × in its folio closes it. One page stays, centred. Folding back keeps the facing note as the ⇄.

Where the option lives

  • Settings › Look › Appearance › Facing pages: Facing pages · One page, kept per device. One page is the phone’s page, wider, with the other note a tap away.
  • ⋯ › Facing pages on the page is the same setting, with a tick. On a phone it’s greyed.
  • It sits beside the desktop’s Split setting, whose Facing pages it borrows. If the desktop’s split mode is chosen to follow there too, this setting could become Split’s own value on a touch screen.

For

  • The room goes to the writing. A chapter and its notes is the case a bigger screen helps most.
  • The fold means something: a book’s gutter. Folding and unfolding turn the phone’s ⇄ into a facing page and back, so nothing is lost or moved.
  • It builds on what’s decided: Facing pages (the gutter, the ribbon, the folio), the phone’s other note, Settings over the window.
  • The phone’s stack stays whole. It comes in as a drawer, by the same back word.

Against

  • The list is never in sight while you write. Finding a note always goes through the drawer, which is one tap more than 1 or 2.
  • Two leaves of 360 are each a phone’s width, so the words are set smaller than on the tablet’s one page.
  • The phone has never had a split: this brings two open notes, an active one, a ribbon and folios to a touch screen. That’s more to learn.
  • On a wide screen the facing page stays put when you open a note, but folded, the phone’s rule (the note you came from) holds. That’s two rules for the other note.
